# Best Games Game Research

Use this as stage three. Research each selected game individually, then hand the writer a compact, source-backed understanding of what playing it involves and why it deserves its place.

## Inputs and output

Read `research/discovery.md`, `research/selection.md`, and the article brief. Write one `research/game-research.md` or one file per game inside `research/games/`. Keep exact source URLs with each game and record the checked date.

## Research each game

Verify identity and availability first: title, universe ID, official Roblox URL, creator, resolving universe/place, playable current experience, square icon, a current landscape thumbnail, and any Bloxodes stats link. Record the exact thumbnail source page and original image URL, its dimensions/crop, a useful alt text, and where it should appear in the section. Then research the actual experience using official descriptions, creator updates, reliable guides or wikis, gameplay footage when useful, and other sources that expose the real loop. Use statistics only as context; they are not a substitute for understanding the game or its quality.

For every selected game, capture:

- the opening situation and what a player does minute to minute;
- the main loop, progression, objectives, maps or rounds, and what creates replay value;
- mechanics, pacing, atmosphere, puzzle or survival pressure, teamwork, communication, or solo strengths that distinguish it;
- the best reason to recommend it, who will enjoy it, likely frustration or grind, onboarding issues, and meaningful content warnings;
- current-state evidence: recent update or creator activity when available, signs that the experience remains active, and any unresolved abandonment or access concern;
- a clear quality judgment grounded in the loop, pacing, polish, replayability, and fit for the target query; do not reduce this to visits or competitor mentions;
- one or two useful comparisons to another selected game when the contrast is real;
- one or two specific details worth writing and one or two generic details to leave out;
- facts that can safely become article copy and facts that must not be claimed;
- source URLs, image provenance, and confidence for each important claim.

## Voice boundary

The final article should sound like a Roblox player making recommendations, but research must not fabricate personal play sessions. Supply concrete experiential detail so the writer can use a confident editorial voice. If a claim is not verified, mark it as uncertain or omit it.

## Completion standard

Every selected game needs a complete record before writing begins. Do not leave a game represented only by player counts, a genre label, or a generic “best for” tag. Flag stale, contradictory, or missing information for editorial handling. A selected game is not ready for writing until its square card image and landscape article thumbnail are both verified.
