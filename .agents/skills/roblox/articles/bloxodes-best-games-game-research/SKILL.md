# Best Games Game Research

You research each selected game on its own and hand the writer a compact, source-backed picture of what playing it is like and why it earned its spot. Done means every selected game has a complete record and verified images, so the writer never has to guess.

This is stage three, after competitor, discovery and selection research and before writing.

## Inputs and output

Read `research/discovery.md`, `research/selection.md` and the article brief. Write one `research/game-research.md`, or one file per game in `research/games/`. Keep exact source URLs with each game and record the checked date.

## Research each game

### 1. Identity and availability first

Verify: title, universe ID, official Roblox URL, creator, resolving universe and place, a playable current experience, square icon, a current landscape thumbnail, and any Bloxodes stats link.

For the thumbnail, record the exact source page and original image URL, its dimensions or crop, useful alt text, and where it should go in the section.

### 2. Then the actual experience

Use official descriptions, creator updates, reliable guides or wikis, gameplay footage when useful, and other sources that show the real loop. Stats are context only. They don't replace understanding the game or its quality.

For every selected game, capture:

- the opening situation and what a player does minute to minute
- the main loop, progression, objectives, maps or rounds, and what makes it replayable
- the mechanics, pacing, atmosphere, puzzle or survival pressure, teamwork, communication or solo strengths that set it apart
- the best reason to recommend it, who'll enjoy it, likely frustration or grind, onboarding issues and meaningful content warnings
- current-state evidence: recent updates or creator activity when available, signs it's still active, and any open abandonment or access concern
- a clear quality judgment based on the loop, pacing, polish, replayability and fit for the query. Don't reduce it to visits or competitor mentions.
- one or two useful comparisons to another selected game, when the contrast is real
- one or two specific details worth writing, and one or two generic details to leave out
- facts that can safely go into the article, and facts that must not be claimed
- source URLs, image provenance and confidence for each important claim

## Write the record for the writer

The final article should sound like a Roblox player recommending games to a friend (see `.agents/skills/bloxodes-voice/SKILL.md`). Your record is the raw material, so make it easy to write from:

- **Give concrete, experiential detail** so the writer can use a confident editorial voice: what you see, do and worry about in the first few minutes.
- **Write facts as plain player statements.** "The lights cut out every two minutes and you have to restart the generator in the dark," not "features a dynamic lighting mechanic."
- **Suggest the hook** for each section: the one detail that would make a reader want to open the game.
- **Keep doubt separate.** Confidence levels and source notes go in their own fields. If a claim isn't verified, mark it uncertain or leave it out.
- **Never fabricate personal play sessions.** Research supplies detail, not invented experience.

## Done when

- Every selected game has a complete record before writing starts. No game is represented only by player counts, a genre label or a generic "best for" tag.
- Stale, contradictory or missing information is flagged for editorial handling.
- A selected game isn't ready for writing until both its square card image and its landscape article thumbnail are verified.
