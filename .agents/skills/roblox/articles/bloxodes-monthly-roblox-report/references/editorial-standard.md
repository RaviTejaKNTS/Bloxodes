# Monthly Roblox editorial standard

## Purpose

Make the reader understand what happened in Roblox during one month and why it was interesting. Statistics support the story; they are not the story.

## Story selection

Lead with the most surprising defensible observation, usually one game with an unmistakable full-period path. Prefer:

- a new breakout with sustained growth;
- a genre movement repeated across matching weekdays;
- a recognizable event rhythm;
- a sharp but sustained cool-down;
- an old game returning to attention;
- a meaningful contrast, such as a genre weakening while its largest game remains stable.

Reject:

- sample counts as headlines;
- generic totals with no human meaning;
- start-day versus end-day league tables;
- tiny games winning only by percentage;
- one unexplained peak;
- platform-wide claims from a selected set;
- facts that answer no reader question.

## Narrative shape

Use one H1 and at most three main H2 sections:

1. the month's strongest game story;
2. the wider genre, event, or community pattern;
3. durable climbers versus cool-downs, ending with concise platform/community context.

The exact structure may vary with the evidence. Do not force the previous month's story onto new data.

Open with a concrete surprise. End with a synthesis, not an internal watchlist or questions for the next report.

## Language

Write for ordinary Roblox players:

- short paragraphs;
- short, direct sentences;
- concrete game names, dates, and comparisons;
- “players online at the same time” instead of `CCU`;
- “typical weekly change” instead of statistical jargon;
- “cooled” or “lost momentum,” never “died.”

Avoid `cohort`, `coverage`, `opening window`, `closing window`, `integrity review`, `sample management`, and other internal terms in public copy.

Use `coincided with`, `landed alongside`, or `the timing matches` for event relationships. Never say an update, algorithm change, meme, or community reaction caused a player movement unless a strong source establishes causation.

## Charts

Use three or four editorial figures at most. Every chart needs:

- a neutral, human title;
- one sentence explaining how to read it;
- a small source line;
- real numeric arrays in code;
- accessible tooltips or labels;
- line patterns or direct labels in addition to color for multi-series charts.

Useful figures:

1. a breakout game's daily average line with verified event markers;
2. a horizontal genre bar using median same-weekday change;
3. indexed event-wave lines where each game's monthly average equals 100;
4. normalized full-period lines for cooling games.

Do not create image-generated charts. Do not surround the page with metric cards. A subtle figure boundary is acceptable when it helps the chart read inside the article.

## Public endnote

Keep methodology to one short endnote. Adapt:

> We used Bloxodes' repeated readings of public Roblox player counts from [dates] and averaged them by day. The figures show players online at the same time, not unique people. Events and news are linked to their original sources. Incomplete collection dates were excluded from trend calculations.

## Source discipline

- Link primary announcements and official game/update pages inline.
- Attribute company test results as company-reported.
- Attribute allegations and include the relevant response.
- Treat forum posts, Reddit, memes, fan art, and videos as qualitative community signals only.
- Never convert selective discussion into a sentiment percentage.
- Do not include a news item merely because it happened during the month; it must matter to players or explain the wider environment.

## Visual direction

The page should resemble a restrained magazine or newspaper feature inside the Bloxodes shell:

- comfortable reading column;
- strong headline and standfirst;
- continuous prose;
- charts placed after the paragraphs that introduce them;
- no dashboard chrome, KPI grid, decorative hero, card wall, or internal-preview badge.

Keep technical `noindex` behavior invisible to readers.

Each report also gets one restrained 1200×630 feature PNG for the reports archive and social previews. Show only one plain `Roblox <Month> Stats Report` context line, the approved headline, Bloxodes wordmark, one meaningful lead metric, and one unlabeled sparkline from the real lead chart series. Do not add any other eyebrow, separate month label, chart caption, axis, date, source label, divider, grid, badge, or decorative copy. Do not use AI-generated art, stock decoration, game screenshots, decorative gradients, or fake charts. Do not repeat the feature image inside the report body where it would duplicate the headline.
