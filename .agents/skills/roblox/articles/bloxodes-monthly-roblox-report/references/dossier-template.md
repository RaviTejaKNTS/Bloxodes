# Monthly Report Evidence Dossier Template

The dossier is the factual contract for the report. Fill in every section, then check it against `analysis.json` and primary sources before anyone writes the page.

Save the finished dossier to `tmp/content-workspace/roblox/reports/YYYY-MM/brief.md`.

## One-sentence editorial thesis

The most important human story, with no method language. Write it the way you'd tell a friend what happened on Roblox this month.

## Why this is the lead

Explain why the observation is surprising, defensible and meaningful at its scale. Name the other leads you considered and why they come second.

## Recommended story flow

### Opening

The hook, the exact facts to use and where the first chart goes.

### Main section 1

The wider genre or event pattern and its evidence.

### Main section 2

Climbers, cool-downs, contrasts and the platform or community ending.

Keep the finished page to no more than three H2 sections.

## Exact game evidence

One subsection per serious story candidate:

- game name, universe ID, Bloxodes slug, genre, creation date
- usable daily average
- observed low and high dates and values
- median same-weekday weekly change
- share of same-weekday comparisons that rose
- strongest and weakest seven-day averages
- weekday pattern
- relevant Roblox events and update dates
- safe interpretation
- interpretations the evidence doesn't support
- Bloxodes stats link and outside sources that back it up

## Genre evidence

A compact table:

| Genre | Stable games | Typical same-weekday change | Share that rose | Combined daily average | Notes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|

Explain scale, persistence, rotation inside the genre, and any thin genre that should be left out.

## Cool-down evidence

Use full-period or rolling paths:

| Game | Monthly daily average | Typical weekly change | Strongest 7-day avg | Weakest 7-day avg | Safe wording |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|

Name a control or counterexample when it stops an overbroad genre claim.

## Platform, news and community context

For every candidate item:

- event date
- primary or reputable source URL
- why it matters, in plain words
- the exact claim allowed
- attribution required
- connection to the game data, if any
- the causal claim that's explicitly forbidden

Sort them into:

- official Roblox changes
- major community or creator developments
- legal, safety or business news
- individual-game fandom or sentiment signals

## Chart specifications

For every proposed figure:

- title
- chart type
- exact games or genres
- date range
- field or calculation
- normalization or index explanation
- event markers and their source
- caption
- accessibility treatment
- the claim the chart supports
- the misleading reading to avoid

Four figures at most.

## Feature image specification

- a short social headline, split by hand into one to three lines
- the plain `Roblox <Month> Stats Report` context line
- one meaningful lead metric in plain language
- the exact report series used for the sparkline
- accessible alt text
- the public PNG path

The feature image uses the report's approved lead observation. Don't introduce a new fact or visual claim just for social sharing.

## Public writing and design guardrails

Copy the relevant constraints from `references/editorial-standard.md`, point the writer to `.agents/skills/bloxodes-voice/SKILL.md` for voice, and add any warnings specific to this month.

## Facts to avoid

Rejected metrics, anomalies, unverified peaks, weak sources, causal claims, and comparisons that are tempting but misleading.

## Sources used

Group by:

- Bloxodes and Roblox data
- Roblox primary sources
- official game sources
- independent reporting
- community context

Put each URL next to the exact fact it supports.

## Sources found but not used

Name irrelevant, duplicate, thin, blocked or unreliable sources and why you left them out.

## Limitations

Cover:

- excluded dates or incomplete samples
- the selected-game scope
- players online at the same time versus unique people
- private retention, recommendation or revenue data we can't see
- event timing not proving causation
- incomplete measurement of community sentiment
- limits of genre classification

## Suggested public endnote

One short paragraph only.

## Definition of success

The two or three things a casual reader should remember after reading the page.
